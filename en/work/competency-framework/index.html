From Job Analysis to a Competency-Based HR Framework

Supporting the transition towards competency-based people management in a public-sector organisation

Organizational Development · HR Consulting

I worked on a real consulting project for a mid-sized public-sector organisation moving towards competency-based people management. My role combined substantial hands-on technical delivery with direct professional supervision, from Job Analysis and role profiling to competency-framework adaptation and competency–role alignment.

I later used the consulting case as the basis of my Master's thesis, extending the work independently into role-level learning-needs analysis and the design of a proposed leadership-development intervention.

The challenge

Competency-based people management requires more than a competency dictionary. Before competencies can support selection, development or performance practices, the organisation needs a reliable understanding of what its roles involve and which behaviours are relevant to effective performance.

The consulting project therefore needed to connect:

organisational context → roles → competencies → role requirements

Job Analysis and competency-framework development were partially iterative rather than strictly sequential, with outputs being reviewed and refined as the project progressed.

From employee input to structured role profiles

A Job Analysis questionnaire was used to collect structured information about the work performed across the organisation.

I contributed to the design and refinement of the Job Analysis process and, once responses were available, carried out a substantial part of the consolidation work.

I organised employee questionnaire responses by role, synthesising heterogeneous descriptions into structured information about functions, responsibilities and other relevant job requirements. I then developed and refined job profiles based on that consolidated evidence.

The purpose was not simply to reproduce individual answers. Different employees could describe similar work in different ways, so the task required identifying common responsibilities, resolving differences in wording and creating a coherent role-level representation.

Draft profiles were subsequently reviewed with organisational stakeholders. I supported these validation meetings, captured feedback and clarification points and incorporated agreed changes into the relevant documentation.

The result was a more structured representation of organisational roles that could provide a foundation for subsequent competency-based HR practices.

Adapting the competency framework

The competency framework was adapted and contextualised from existing competency frameworks rather than created from scratch.

I contributed directly to this process by working with competency definitions, reviewing how the emerging framework related to evidence from the Job Analysis and helping identify potential omissions or inconsistencies.

The framework was iteratively refined under professional supervision and with organisational input.

This created a shared competency language connected to the organisation's context rather than a generic list of desirable behaviours.

Connecting competencies to roles

The next step was to determine how the competency framework related to different jobs.

I worked with information on competency relevance by role, consolidated responses at role level and contributed to the correspondence between roles and transversal competencies. I also incorporated revisions arising from the validation process.

The resulting architecture connected:

role profiles → competency requirements → competency–role mapping

The final mapping was part of a supervised and validated consulting process; I did not independently determine the client's competency model or make unilateral decisions about role requirements.

My contribution

As an HR Consulting Technician, I carried out substantial hands-on technical work across the consulting project under the direct supervision of an HR professional. I contributed to Job Analysis design, consolidated employee questionnaire responses by role, synthesised functions and responsibilities, developed and refined job profiles, supported stakeholder-validation meetings, contributed to the adaptation and contextualisation of the competency framework, and worked on competency–role alignment.

My work was reviewed and corrected by the supervising HR professional throughout the process.

Academic extension — from mapping to learning needs

Master's thesis based on the consulting case · Not a second client engagement

I later used the project as the basis of my Master's thesis, where I independently developed the role-level competency-gap / learning-needs analysis.

The analysis compared competencies reflected in the current configuration of roles with competencies identified as strategically relevant to those roles. It was therefore designed to identify role-level development priorities, not to measure individual employees' personal skill deficits.

Leadership competencies emerged as a priority area within that academic analysis.

I then designed a proposed leadership-development intervention and evaluation framework, connecting the identified development needs with learning objectives, content, practical activities, transfer to the workplace and follow-up evaluation.

The intervention remained an academic proposal. It was not implemented or delivered to the client, and no post-intervention outcomes are claimed.

What the work enabled

The professional consulting work created foundations for a more integrated competency-based approach to people management:

structured role documentation · a shared competency language · competency–role alignment

These outputs could support subsequent practices such as competency-based selection, employee development, training and performance-management design.

The academic extension demonstrated how the same architecture could be taken one step further:

mapping → development priorities → intervention design

The case does not claim implementation of the wider competency-management system or measurable organisational impact.

Confidentiality & scope

The client remains fully anonymised.

No original job descriptions, questionnaire responses, interview notes, organisational charts, competency dictionaries, competency–role matrices or internal client data will be displayed.

Any portfolio artefacts representing the methodology will use fictional roles, generic competencies and synthetic data.
